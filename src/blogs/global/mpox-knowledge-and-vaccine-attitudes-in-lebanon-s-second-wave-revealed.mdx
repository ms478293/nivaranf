The global health landscape is in constant flux, continuously challenged by emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases. Among these, mpox (formerly monkeypox) has garnered significant international attention, necessitating a robust understanding of public perception and response to containment strategies. A pivotal study, published in 2026, delves into the knowledge and attitudes surrounding mpox among the Lebanese population during the disease's second wave, offering critical insights into vaccination attitudes that hold profound implications for global public health preparedness.

### The Evolving Mpox Landscape and Public Engagement

Mpox, a viral zoonotic disease, typically presents with flu-like symptoms, swollen lymph nodes, and a characteristic rash. While historically endemic to parts of Central and West Africa, its global spread in 2022 marked a significant epidemiological shift, prompting the World Health Organization to declare it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. The subsequent waves of infection in various regions underscored the need for not only rapid diagnostic and treatment capabilities but also effective public health communication and community engagement strategies. Understanding how populations perceive the threat, acquire information, and respond to preventive measures, particularly vaccination, is paramount to controlling outbreaks and mitigating future risks.

Lebanon, a nation grappling with complex socio-economic and political challenges, presents a unique context for studying public health responses. The country's healthcare system has faced immense strain, and public trust in institutions has been tested. These factors inevitably shape how health information is received and how individuals make decisions regarding their health, including vaccine acceptance. The study's focus on the Lebanese population during the second wave of mpox offers a window into how a community navigates a health crisis amidst broader societal pressures, providing valuable lessons for other regions facing similar complexities.

### Unpacking Knowledge Gaps and Vaccination Attitudes

The research employed a comprehensive approach to assess the Lebanese population's understanding of mpox and their inclinations towards vaccination. It aimed to identify specific areas of knowledge deficit, gauge the prevalence of misconceptions, and explore the underlying factors influencing vaccine hesitancy or acceptance. Such studies typically involve large-scale surveys, capturing demographic data, information sources, perceived risk, and attitudes towards public health interventions. The findings reveal a nuanced picture of public engagement with the mpox crisis.

One of the study's key revelations pertains to significant knowledge gaps within the population. While general awareness of mpox might have been present, detailed understanding of its transmission routes, symptom presentation, severity, and the specific populations at higher risk remained inconsistent. Misconceptions, often fueled by rapidly circulating information on social media, contributed to a distorted perception of the disease. For instance, some individuals might have underestimated the virus's potential for severe outcomes, while others might have held inaccurate beliefs about its primary modes of spread, leading to either complacency or undue panic. These knowledge deficits directly impact risk perception and, consequently, the willingness to adopt preventive behaviors, including vaccination.

Attitudes towards mpox vaccination emerged as another critical area of investigation. The study found a spectrum of responses, ranging from strong acceptance to considerable hesitancy. Factors contributing to vaccine hesitancy were multifaceted. Concerns about the safety and efficacy of the mpox vaccine, often stemming from a lack of clear, consistent information or the influence of anti-vaccine sentiments prevalent during the broader pandemic era, played a significant role. Trust in health authorities and government institutions also proved to be a powerful determinant. In contexts where public trust is eroded, even well-intentioned public health campaigns can struggle to gain traction. Furthermore, the perceived necessity of the vaccine, influenced by an individual's assessment of their own risk, was a strong predictor of acceptance. Those who believed their risk of contracting mpox was low were less inclined to seek vaccination.

### Influencing Factors and Information Pathways

The research further illuminated how various demographic and socio-economic factors correlated with knowledge levels and vaccination attitudes. For instance, higher levels of education often corresponded with better knowledge about mpox and a greater propensity for vaccine acceptance, although this was not universally true. Age, urban versus rural residence, and access to reliable information sources also played significant roles. Younger demographics, often more exposed to social media, were sometimes found to be more susceptible to misinformation, while older populations might have relied more on traditional media or direct advice from healthcare providers.

Crucially, the study highlighted the profound impact of information pathways. Social media platforms, while facilitating rapid dissemination of information, also served as conduits for misinformation and disinformation, complicating public health efforts. The absence of clear, authoritative, and consistent messaging from official health channels created a vacuum that was often filled by unverified claims. Conversely, healthcare professionals emerged as highly trusted sources of information, underscoring their indispensable role in public health communication. Engaging local doctors, pharmacists, and community health workers in vaccine promotion and education campaigns proved to be more effective than broad, top-down directives.

### Strategic Public Health Interventions and Global Implications

The findings from Lebanon offer actionable insights for designing more effective public health interventions globally. Firstly, the imperative for targeted, culturally sensitive communication strategies cannot be overstated. Generic public health messages often fail to resonate with diverse populations. Campaigns must be tailored to address specific knowledge gaps, debunk prevalent myths, and build trust within particular communities. This requires a deep understanding of local beliefs, communication preferences, and existing social structures.

Secondly, strengthening the role of healthcare professionals as trusted communicators is vital. Investing in their training to effectively address patient concerns, provide accurate information, and counter misinformation can significantly boost vaccine confidence. Moreover, fostering transparent and consistent communication from public health authorities is essential to rebuild and maintain public trust, especially in environments where skepticism is high.

Thirdly, proactive strategies to combat misinformation are critical. This includes collaborating with social media platforms to identify and flag false information, promoting media literacy among the public, and ensuring that reliable, evidence-based information is easily accessible and presented in an understandable format. Learning from the mpox experience in Lebanon, global health bodies can develop frameworks for rapidly deploying counter-narratives to emerging health threats.

Finally, the study underscores the universal challenge of ensuring equitable access to vaccines and health services. Even with willingness to vaccinate, logistical barriers, cost, or geographical distance can impede uptake. Addressing these systemic issues is fundamental to achieving widespread protection against infectious diseases. The lessons learned from Lebanon's experience with mpox during its second wave are not isolated; they resonate across the globe, offering valuable guidance for countries navigating similar public health crises. Understanding the intricate interplay of knowledge, attitudes, and socio-political contexts is essential for building resilient health systems capable of responding effectively to future pandemics and protecting global health security.

In conclusion, the research from Lebanon provides a compelling case study on the complexities of public health engagement during an infectious disease outbreak. It reinforces the critical need for continuous surveillance of public perceptions, adaptive communication strategies, and a concerted effort to build trust between communities and health authorities. As the world continues to face the threat of emerging pathogens, these insights are indispensable for fostering informed decision-making, promoting preventive behaviors, and ultimately safeguarding global well-being.
