The emergency in Nepal is extending well beyond the first days of rescue. Families need dependable water supplies, working health services and somewhere safe to stay while they decide what comes next.

This briefing, published on 14 September, draws on UNICEF’s 8 September situation report and its early September water and sanitation update. The figures below belong to those reporting dates; assessments can change as access improves.

## The latest published assessment

UNICEF’s 8 September report estimates that the floods affected **84,270 people, including 32,021 children**, across six districts. It records damage to **46 water supply systems, 23 schools and seven health facilities**. As of 7 September, 3,630 displaced people were staying in 37 holding centres.

The report also describes progress: UNICEF-supported repairs had restored water systems serving about 17,500 people, while essential water, sanitation and hygiene supplies had reached 48,600. These are results reported by UNICEF and its response partners. [Read UNICEF’s situation report, 8 September](https://www.unicef.org/nepal/media/31391/file/UNICEF-Nepal-Situation-Report%203-8-Sept-2026.pdf.pdf).

## Safe water is a continuing need

In a statement dated 3 September and published on 4 September, UNICEF described damaged water networks and sanitation facilities alongside crowded temporary accommodation. Its response combines immediate supplies with work to repair infrastructure and share verified hygiene information. The agency stressed that rebuilding water systems needs sustained effort and better protection against future floods. [Read UNICEF’s water and sanitation update](https://www.unicef.org/nepal/press-releases/more-one-week-nepals-floods-thousands-children-remain-without-safe-water-and-face).

For supporters following the response, the useful question is what people can reliably access each day. A delivery is one milestone. A functioning service that families can keep using is another.
